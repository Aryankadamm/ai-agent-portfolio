# Clinic discovery and pilot plan

## Discovery interviews

Talk to about ten clinic owners, managers, or receptionists in one city and one specialty. Ask them to describe recent work before showing the product.

1. Walk me through yesterday's enquiries and appointments.
2. Which questions repeat most often, and through which channel?
3. How many calls/messages were missed last week? How do you know?
4. Which duties require a person physically at the clinic?
5. What does the clinic currently spend on scheduling, calls, messages, software, and front-desk staff?
6. When is the next receptionist hire or staffing change planned?
7. What would make you stop a pilot immediately?
8. Who can approve a trial and what patient-data rules must it follow?

Ask for anonymized counts and workflow examples. Do not collect patient identities or clinical histories during discovery.

## Pilot design

- **Baseline:** record 2 weeks of staff time by task, enquiry volume, response time, booked visits, attended visits, and existing software/telephony costs.
- **Assisted period:** start with admin FAQ and scheduling only, with a visible human fallback. Use synthetic data in a prototype; use real patient data only after a clinic-approved privacy and security review.
- **Review:** compare workload and outcomes with the baseline. Note tasks still requiring a human, user complaints, incorrect answers, and every human handoff.
- **Decision:** continue only if the clinic finds the workflow safe and useful and the measured economics support the agreed price.

## Suggested success measures

- Correct answer rate on a clinic-approved admin FAQ set.
- Availability accuracy compared with the actual schedule.
- Enquiry-to-booking conversion and attended-appointment rate.
- Human handoff rate and missed handoffs.
- Paid staff minutes removed, or documented hire avoided.
- Total ongoing usage and support cost.
- Patient opt-out, complaint, and correction rate.

Set thresholds with the clinic before the pilot. Do not invent a success percentage after seeing the results.

## Go-to-market evidence

For an early sale, prepare a short demo, a one-page workflow map, actual pilot measurements, clear one-time and recurring cost terms, data handling details, and an honest limitations list. A demo alone does not establish willingness to pay or savings.
