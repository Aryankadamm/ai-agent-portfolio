# Product brief: ClinicFlow AI

## Problem hypothesis

Small outpatient clinics may spend staff time answering repeated administrative questions and coordinating appointments. A clinic may value automation if it measurably reduces paid admin hours, prevents a planned hire, or recovers appointments. The hypothesis is unproven until clinic interviews and a measured pilot support it.

## First target to interview

Start with one-doctor independent clinics in one city. Prefer clinics where the owner can approve a small operational pilot and can show anonymized appointment and call counts. Choose one specialty only after interviews show a repeated, mostly administrative workflow. Do not assume every clinic has the same front-desk tasks.

## User and buyer

- **Buyer:** clinic owner or practice manager.
- **Daily operator:** receptionist or clinic administrator.
- **Patient:** asks for clinic logistics or an appointment.

The receptionist is a workflow stakeholder. A responsible design should measure which work is removed, which work shifts, and which in-person duties still need a person.

## Initial job to be done

“When a patient asks about clinic logistics or wants an appointment, help them get an accurate administrative answer or find a real available slot, and hand off anything outside that scope.”

## MVP boundary

### Included in this portfolio demo

- Fictional clinic profile and schedule.
- Admin FAQ lookup for approved clinic details.
- Read-only appointment availability lookup.
- Confirmed demo booking through a separate form.
- Small dashboard and synthetic schedule records.

### Excluded from this demo

- Medical questions, diagnosis, treatment advice, and clinical triage.
- Phone or WhatsApp integration.
- Patient record or EMR integration.
- Payment processing, prescription, insurance, or emergency dispatch.
- Claims that the product replaces a receptionist or guarantees savings.

## Value hypothesis and measurement

Capture a baseline before automation and compare it with a pilot period. Use clinic records, not estimates from a demo screen.

```text
Verified monthly cash savings
= paid admin hours actually removed × clinic's actual hourly employment cost
  + other documented recurring costs removed
  - monthly product infrastructure and service costs

Payback months
= one-time implementation price ÷ verified monthly cash savings
```

Track separately:

- paid receptionist hours removed or a planned hire avoided;
- routine enquiries handled without staff intervention;
- missed enquiries that became attended appointments;
- cancellations and no-shows;
- phone, messaging, model, hosting, and support costs;
- patient and staff experience, including handoff failures.

Recovered appointment revenue is not the same as salary savings. Estimate it using contribution margin and observed attendance, and report it separately.

## One-time offer constraint

A one-time setup price does not eliminate recurring infrastructure costs. Before quoting a permanent price, document who owns and pays for model usage, hosting, phone routing, WhatsApp messages, updates, and support. A clinic-owned account can keep usage charges transparent. Define exactly how long setup support and updates are included.

## Evidence gates

1. **Discovery:** speak with clinic operators; map the work and quantify actual labor and call volumes.
2. **Prototype:** demonstrate a narrow, approved admin workflow with synthetic data.
3. **Pilot:** measure baseline and assisted workflow in a real clinic under an approved data and privacy arrangement.
4. **Commercial test:** present a clear price and operating-cost schedule; record a real purchase decision.

Do not claim proven ROI, customer traction, receptionist replacement, or production readiness until that evidence exists.
